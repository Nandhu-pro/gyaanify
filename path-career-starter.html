Choosing a path

Popular entry routes include cloud engineering, DevOps, security operations (SOC), software development, data and AI. Each needs a different mix of skills, but all share the basics: networking, Linux and a scripting language such as Python.

Try a short hands-on lab in two or three areas before committing. What you enjoy practising is usually what you'll stick with.

Key takeaways

  • Learn the shared basics first
  • Try before you commit
  • Follow what you enjoy practising
Lesson 2 of 42 min read

Certifications that matter

Certifications help you learn in a structured way and show employers you know the fundamentals. Good starting points are Azure Fundamentals (AZ-900), Security Fundamentals (SC-900) and CompTIA Security+.

Pick one certification that matches your chosen path and finish it, rather than collecting many. Pair every certification with a small project that uses what you learned.

Key takeaways

  • Start with one fundamentals certification
  • Match it to your path
  • Always pair it with a project
Lesson 3 of 42 min read

Building a portfolio

A portfolio is proof you can do the work. Put two or three small, complete projects on GitHub, each with a clear README that explains the problem, what you built and what you learned.

Share short posts about your progress on LinkedIn. Consistent, honest learning in public builds credibility faster than a long list of course badges.

Key takeaways

  • Two or three finished projects beat ten unfinished ones
  • Write clear READMEs
  • Learn in public
Lesson 4 of 42 min read

Acing interviews

Prepare stories about your projects using a simple structure: the situation, what you did and the result. Interviewers want to hear how you think and solve problems.

Review fundamentals, practise explaining them out loud, and ask thoughtful questions about the team. It's fine to say "I don't know, but here's how I would find out."

Key takeaways

  • Tell clear project stories
  • Explain your thinking out loud
  • Honesty beats guessing
